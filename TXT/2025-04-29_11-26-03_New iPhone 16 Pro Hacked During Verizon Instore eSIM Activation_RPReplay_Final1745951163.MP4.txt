* UI Redressing and Tapjacking Mechanisms

### 1. Definition of Terms

* **Clickjacking:** A malicious technique of tricking a user into clicking on a concealed logical layer. This is a form of **UI Redressing**, where the attacker uses transparent or opaque layers to hide the true functional interface.
* **Tapjacking:** A mobile-specific subset of clickjacking. It involves the use of a deceptive overlay (such as a "phantom" app or notification) that captures touch events intended for the visible UI and redirects them to a hidden, underlying UI.

### 2. Conceptual Framework of the Attack

The exploit described—where a user interacts with a perceived notification while unknowingly modifying system settings—operates on the following technical principles:

* **Layer Orchestration:** The attacker utilizes a "Top-most" window attribute (where permitted by the OS) to render an invisible or deceptive graphical layer over the system’s legitimate settings interface.
* **Touch Event Redirection:** The operating system’s window manager registers a "Touch Down" event. If the malicious overlay is programmed to be "touch-transparent" or if it is positioned to intercept the coordinate ($x, y$) of the user's finger, the action is executed on the hidden layer.
* **Visual Discrepancy:** There is a total decoupling between the **Visual Representation** (what the user sees) and the **Execution Logic** (the setting being changed).
